# STAT 19000: Data Science Through Experimentation - Grading System

## Overview
This course is built around learning by doing: live experiments, games, and a real capstone
project, rather than lectures and problem sets. The grading system rewards genuine
understanding and honest reasoning, and gives you plenty of room to use AI as a tool, as long
as you can explain and defend your own work.

## Grade Distribution

| Component | Percentage | Details |
|---|---|---|
| In-Class Activities & Participation | 25% | Weekly interactive activities (live experiments, games, debates, juries). Graded on engagement and thoughtful participation, not being "right." |
| AI Jury Assignment | 10% | A single graded activity (Week 11) where you evaluate a real AI-generated data analysis and argue whether it holds up. |
| Capstone Project | 45% | Your big project, a real experiment or causal analysis you design and run yourself, developed across Weeks 9 through 13. |
| Capstone Showcase Presentation | 10% | Presenting your capstone's findings, and its limitations, to the class (Week 15). |
| Peer Review Contribution | 10% | Quality of feedback given to classmates during peer review (Week 12). |

---

## In-Class Activities & Participation (25% total)

### How It Works
Nearly every class includes a graded-for-participation activity: a live experiment, a game
(Kahoot-style quizzes, "Spot the Confounder," debate rounds), or a hands-on challenge. You are
graded on genuine engagement and effort, not on getting the "correct" answer, the whole point
is that some of these are designed to fool you.

### AI Usage: In-Class Activities
Not really applicable, these happen live, in class, together. Outside prep (if any) may use
AI freely; you can't "AI" your way through a live coin-flip tournament.

---

## AI Jury Assignment (10%)

You're given a real (deliberately flawed) AI-generated analysis of a dataset. In your assigned
jury, you'll argue whether the analysis's conclusion should be "convicted" (rejected) or
"acquitted" (holds up), and why. Graded on the specificity and accuracy of your reasoning.

**AI Usage: This Assignment.** Using AI to help you evaluate the provided analysis is
explicitly fine and expected, you're being asked to out-reason an AI, and using other tools to
help you do that is fair game.

---

## Capstone Project (45% total)

### Structure
- **Week 9:** Proposal (question, dataset, planned approach)
- **Week 10:** Build Sprint I, execution
- **Weeks 11 to 12:** Continued build, peer review and revision
- **Week 13:** Final analysis submitted

### Grading Breakdown
| Component | Weight | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Question & Design | 20% | Is the question answerable? Is the approach appropriate? |
| Execution | 40% | Correct implementation, honest handling of messy data, appropriate interpretation |
| Awareness of Limitations | 20% | Does the analysis honestly identify what it can't tell you? |
| Incorporation of Peer Feedback | 20% | Did the final version meaningfully respond to Week 12 review? |

### AI Usage: Capstone
**Allowed, like a tool you're expected to use well.** You're free to use AI for Python syntax,
debugging, brainstorming approaches, or even drafting parts of your write-up. What matters is that you understand and can defend
everything you submit, you may occasionally be asked a quick question about your own project
in class to confirm the reasoning is genuinely yours. Significant AI use should be briefly
noted in your submission, more as good practice than as a restriction.

---

## Capstone Showcase Presentation (10% total, Week 15)

### Format (5 minutes plus questions)
1. **The Question** (1 min)
2. **What You Found** (2 min), explained without jargon
3. **What It Doesn't Tell You** (1 min): honest limitations
4. **Questions** (1 min)

Any tools may be used to prepare slides or practice delivery. The conclusions must be your own
and consistent with your disclosed capstone work.

---

## Peer Review Contribution (10% total)
Graded on the specificity and usefulness of the feedback you give during Week 12's peer review
session. Vague praise doesn't count; identifying a real gap or suggesting a concrete fix does.

---

## Extra Credit Opportunities

### 1. Real-World Fallacy Writeup (up to +3% final grade)
Find and write up a real misleading statistical claim from the news or social media. Due
anytime before Week 12.

### 2. Dataset Contribution (up to +2% final grade)
Propose and clean a real dataset for use in a future offering of the course, with documentation.

---

## Academic Integrity

### Collaboration Policy
- **Encouraged:** Discussing concepts, debugging together, peer review, brainstorming approaches
- **Not Allowed:** Submitting identical capstone analyses, having someone else complete your
  work for you

### General AI Policy
AI is a tool you're expected to learn to use well and evaluate critically. You're free to use
it throughout the course, including on the capstone. The one place we ask you to lean on it less is
during live in-class activities, simply because the whole point of those is what happens when
it's just you and the data. Across every component, the standard is the same: you should always
be able to explain your own reasoning if asked.

### Academic Dishonesty
Handled per Purdue's standard academic integrity policy. First offense: no credit on the
component and a required meeting with the instructor. Further offenses referred per university
policy.
